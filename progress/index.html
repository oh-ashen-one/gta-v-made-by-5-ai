The B frames show the same arid Southern-California world: dense, weathered and full of story, with layered aerial haze and lighting that its geometry backs up. Long cast shadows, contact shadows, sky reflections in car paint and rim-lit silhouettes make the sun position believable. The A frames are a Singapore-themed city with shophouses, supertrees and a Marina-Bay-style hotel, and they share the same weaknesses across all four. Large surfaces are clean or untextured: asphalt is a flat dark plane, paving repeats perfectly and roofs are flat single colours. Population and prop density are very low. Landmarks look like placeholders (wireframe-looking supertrees, cloned extruded towers). There are artifacts: hollow ring-shaped tree canopies from above, a pure-black median face, and a bare flat horizon where the world seems to end. The lighting gets the warm colour grade, bloom and some haze right, but it has few shadow-casting objects in the foreground, so it never shows the sun direction the way B does. A's best assets are its warm golden-hour grade and the ornate shophouse facades with lit interiors. The fastest wins would be material variation and wear on asphalt and paving, street furniture and vehicles that cast long shadows, more pedestrians, and replacing the greybox roofs and clone towers. None of the A frames wows. Two B frames genuinely do: pair 1's weathered liquor-store hero shot and pair 2's flare-lit, backlit desert inn. Pair 3's flat midday grass and pair 4's smudgy clouds keep those B frames just short.
